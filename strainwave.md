# Strainwave: The Force Layer of the Physical World

*The business thesis, product ambition, and way I work. Public overview, October 2026.*

I’m building **Strainwave** toward a force layer for the physical world: making what objects carry measurable, understandable, and usable by people and machines. **My current bet is radar.**

The ambition is infrastructure. The work today is research and development: establish what the technology can reliably do, make deployment simple, and find the customer outcome that justifies a business. A broad vision earns its place through specific evidence.

## The problem I want to solve

Knowing where something is and knowing what it carries are different questions. A picture of a structure does not, by itself, tell you the load through each part. A useful force measurement also needs context: what was measured, under which conditions, and whether the result is reliable enough for the decision being made.

Strainwave starts with that decision. What would someone do differently if they knew the load? Would they redistribute it, change an operation, inspect an asset, or give a machine better information? If the reading does not change a useful decision, more sensors are not a business.

## The product ambition

The **sticker ambition** is a measurement point that can be added to an existing object bearing weight. I judge it by how easy it is to deploy and use.

The target has two workflows: a point set up once with information about the object or a known load, and a point that can report current load on an unfamiliar, already-loaded object. Those are distinct research problems. Neither universal compatibility nor automatic absolute-load measurement is a claim made here.

The longer-term goal is shared reading infrastructure serving many points, with software that makes the measurements useful to humans and machines. The technical implementation remains private.

## The economic bet

The question is not just how cheaply we can make a tag. It is how cheaply a customer can obtain a useful, dependable measurement across an entire deployment.

**Multiplexing** means sharing reading infrastructure across many measurement points. My hypothesis is that this can change the economics enough to make previously uneconomic measurement worthwhile. That is what I mean by **price inversion**: create demand by changing what is practical to measure.

Three requirements have to hold together:

- **Better economics:** compare the complete job, including installation, calibration, qualification, and maintenance.
- **Many points on shared infrastructure:** useful coverage and readings in the intended environment.
- **Easy deployment and use:** less work for the customer to obtain and act on the measurement.

A cheap component or a successful bench reading does not establish all three. The comparison must be against the cheapest credible way to achieve the same customer outcome, including conventional sensing or a simpler workflow.

## How this becomes a business

The customer buys a better decision, an easier operation, or a capability they could not previously afford. The hardware is how we enable that value.

Pricing, packaging, and the commercial model remain open. The test is whether the product creates enough customer value to support a durable business. Any recurring charge would need continuing value behind it, such as useful measurement history, dependable operation, or better decisions.

I want Strainwave to create valuable measurement capabilities that open new markets, and to beat the best available alternative where a market already exists. **A first market has not been selected.** Construction, industrial assets, and machine interaction are possible application areas, not announced products or customer deployments. I want the capability evidence to guide the first application.

The path is to prove a useful measurement, prove its economics and deployment in a specific setting, and prove that a customer acts on it. Then earn the right to expand the layer.

## Why the layer matters

A question behind this effort is whether useful intelligence can live in the environment as well as in the machine observing it. The environment could expose measurements that different people and machines can read, reducing what each machine has to infer independently.

That connects Strainwave to robotics and physical AI. The destination is force information that supports action; the near-term work is the measurement and deployment capability required to get there. This is a direction, not a claim that Strainwave already provides a universal machine interface.

## How I think and work

- **First principles before frameworks.** Identify the actual customer decision and the binding constraint. Separate a physical limit from a constraint introduced by our design.
- **Route around avoidable constraints.** Remove a requirement, delete an operation, change the architecture, or run the experiment that resolves the uncertainty.
- **Facts and assumptions stay separate.** Published research, calculations, simulations, and physical measurements answer different questions. Agreement between agents does not turn a model into a measurement.
- **Independent refutation is part of building.** I direct AI coding and research agents under a written verification doctrine. Their reports are inputs; decisive claims need independent checks against the actual source or result.
- **Customer outcomes set the benchmark.** Compare alternatives on the same job. Do not select arbitrary targets because they make a design look good.
- **Preserve failures and decisions.** Record what failed, why a decision changed, and what evidence would reverse it. A convincing narrative cannot substitute for a valid result.

## What would make it defensible

A novel mechanism or a low component cost is not, by itself, a durable business advantage.

The advantages worth earning are dependable measurements, simple deployment, trusted operating history, and integrations that people use. Each has to accumulate through real use. Strainwave’s force-layer ambition is a reason to pursue those advantages, not proof that a moat or standard already exists.

## Current stage and public boundary

Strainwave is in R&D. End-to-end force performance, deployment economics, multi-point coverage, and customer adoption remain validation work. This page makes no claim of a certified product, proven price advantage, or universal compatibility.
